Six weeks. One founder at a time.

01
Week one — I get inside the business

It starts with a 90-minute call where I want to understand how the business really works.

What do you sell? Who buys it? Where does the money come from? What have you tried? What worked? What didn’t? What did you start and abandon? What keeps getting pushed another month?

After that, you give me access to the business: your numbers, ads, email, website, offer, pricing, analytics, calendar, and whatever systems are actually running underneath it.

Then I mostly disappear for a while and read.

I don’t want to spend the first week booking a bunch of discovery calls with you when most of what I need to know is already sitting in the business. I want to see what customers are actually doing, where money is being made or lost, where people are dropping out, which systems are working, which ones are barely working, and where you personally are still carrying work that the business should know how to do without you.

What I look at, in every business
What you sell

How the work gets done
How much still depends on you

There are almost always several things that could be improved. The job is not to make a giant list of them. The job is to understand which one has the most leverage.

Sometimes the founder already knows exactly what that is. Sometimes they think they do, and the numbers tell a different story. That difference between the business as it feels from inside and the business as it actually behaves is often where the most useful answer lives.

02
Week two — I hand you one page

Not because the business is simple, but because I think I should be able to explain what I found without hiding behind a 60-page presentation.

  • ›What I think is actually happening
  • ›What is costing the business the most right now
  • ›What you should stop spending time or money on
  • ›What I believe we should build or change first

Then we go through it together. You don’t have to agree with me because I’m the guy you hired. If you think I’m wrong, tell me I’m wrong. If I missed something important, I want to know before I start touching the business. By the end of that conversation, we both understand what matters most and agree on what I’m going to spend the next four weeks doing.

03
Weeks three to six — then I build it

What that means will be different in every business. It might be:

Rebuilding the path from an ad to a purchase, because the traffic is there and the conversion is broken
Changing the offer or the pricing
Finally building the email system that should have existed two years ago
A retention problem, a subscription problem, or an operational process that still lives in your head
An automation, an AI workflow, or a new sales path
A combination of smaller changes that all have to happen before the larger thing works properly

I don’t believe there is much value in pretending every business has exactly one isolated problem. That has never been my experience. What I do believe is that some problems have much more leverage than others — and if you solve the right one, several of the smaller problems either become easier to solve or stop mattering as much.

So the goal of the six weeks is not, “Brandon fixes one tiny thing and leaves.” The goal is to figure out where the business is most constrained, put my energy there, and fix whatever has to be fixed around it so that the solution actually works when I’m gone.

04
When it ends, the thing belongs to you

I build in your accounts, under your ownership, in a way that you can understand and change after I leave. I don’t want you dependent on some system only I know how to use, and I’m not structuring this around turning six weeks into a permanent retainer.
