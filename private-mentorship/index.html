The real question

What should I actually be working on?

Should you run ads? Redo the website? Build an email funnel? Hire someone so you can finally get some of this work off your plate?

You can make a case for all of them. Each one takes time and money, and you’re the person who has to decide which one comes first.

You know how to work. You’ve already built a business. But when you’re inside it every day—answering questions and dealing with whatever just went wrong—it can be hard to step back far enough to make the call.

That’s where I come in

We decide what deserves your attention. Then work through what it takes to get it done.

We look at the whole business: what you sell, how customers find you, where the money goes, and what keeps landing back on your desk. Then we choose the work that has the strongest reason to come first.

I built The Spinsterz from the ground up, ran it for roughly eighteen years, grew it into a seven-figure business, and eventually sold it. These days, I help other founders figure out what needs fixing and get to work on it.

One of my first questions is: What is it that you actually want?

Maybe it is more consistent sales. Maybe it is keeping more of the money you already make. Maybe it is finishing the day without another evening spent catching up on work nobody else can do. Your answer gives us something real to make decisions against.

The work comes from what we find

The whole business is on the table.

  • Make the offer easier to buyGet specific about who it is for, what they get, and why they should choose it. Find where people hesitate and what they need to understand before they say yes.
  • Get your marketing workingLook at how people find you and what happens next. If they arrive at the website and leave, we find out where and why before spending more to bring them there.
  • Find what is holding things upWalk through where sales stall, work backs up, or decisions wait on you. Then decide whether the fix is a clearer process, a different offer, another person, or something else.
  • Put AI and automation to workFind specific jobs where technology can save time or reduce costs, then make sure the saving is worth the setup.
  • Stop doing work the business has outgrownLook at recurring tasks and ask what they accomplish. If nobody uses the report you spend Friday making, that is a conversation worth having.
  • Finish the work we chooseBreak the decision into what happens next, work through the stuck parts, and give the work a chance to produce a result before another idea takes over.
